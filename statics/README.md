# Statics: start here

[Repository home](../README.md)

Statics asks: **what forces and moments keep an object from accelerating?** This introductory guide starts with algebra and trigonometry and builds toward solving rigid bodies in two dimensions.

**General study material only.** This is not a USC or Midlands Technical College syllabus, exam guide, prerequisite statement, or transfer-credit determination. Match your actual course's notation and requirements when enrolled.

## Conventions and scope

- Right is $+x$, up is $+y$, and counterclockwise moment is positive, unless explicitly changed.
- Ideal rigid bodies do not deform. Ideal frictionless supports exert only the reactions described in the notes.
- A particle has negligible size for the model; a rigid body has size, so force location matters.
- Static equilibrium means zero acceleration and zero angular acceleration, with the body initially at rest. Balanced loads alone do not prove an object was initially at rest.
- Coverage: coplanar forces, particle equilibrium, rigid-body equilibrium, point loads, uniform distributed loads, and ideal supports.
- Not yet covered: trusses, frames/machines, friction, centroids, internal forces, shear/moment diagrams, or 3D equilibrium.

## Before advancing

Can you explain why a pin has two reaction components, why a force through a moment center contributes zero moment, and why a beam needs a moment equation as well as force equations? If not, revisit lessons 3–6.
